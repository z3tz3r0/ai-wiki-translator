# Content policies for translating into Thai Wikipedia

Paraphrased from the th.wiki pages in `../sources.tsv` (read 2026-11-09). Each rule was checked
against a verbatim Thai quote from its page. The page wins over this summary.

## Neutral point of view (วิกิพีเดีย:มุมมองที่เป็นกลาง)

- Opinions are attributed in the text to a named source ("ตามความเห็นของ ..."), never stated in
  Wikipedia's voice. A claim reliable sources dispute is an opinion, not a fact.
- Uncontested facts are stated plainly, not worded to look contested.
- Weight follows the sources: minority views get less space than majority views, extreme
  minority views none (except in articles about them). This covers images, links and
  categories too.
- Name who holds a view (a defined group or a person), not "some say" or "critics".
- No overtly positive or negative wording. Fold debates into the narrative, not into separate
  pro and con sections. Headings and formatting must not favour a side.
- Do not remove sourced content because it looks biased. Rewrite it neutrally.

## Verifiability and sources (การพิสูจน์ยืนยันได้, แหล่งข้อมูลที่น่าเชื่อถือ, งดงานค้นคว้าต้นฉบับ)

- Quotes and anything challenged or likely to be challenged need an inline citation to a
  reliable published source that directly supports it, with page or section where it applies.
  Whoever adds or restores material carries the burden of sourcing it.
- Never cite Wikipedia in any language, or a mirror of it. Use the underlying sources, after
  checking they support the text. Watch for news outlets that copied Wikipedia.
- Prefer reliable secondary sources (reviews, monographs, textbooks, established news). Primary
  sources only for plain descriptive facts, never your own analysis or synthesis of them. No
  unpublished material, no personal experience.
- No conclusion the source does not state. Summarise in your own words without changing the
  meaning or going beyond the source. That is the line between translating and original research.
- Not reliable for facts: op-eds and editorials (only for the writer's opinion), reader
  comments, self-published blogs, web boards, social media, IMDb, open wikis, incomplete
  dissertations. Reprints of one wire story count as one source.
- Exceptional claims need several high-quality sources. Check that an old source has not been
  superseded, especially in fast-moving fields.
- Thai sources are preferred over foreign ones of equal quality and relevance.
- Quoting a non-Thai source: give the original text with the Thai translation (in the text or a
  footnote). Translations by reliable sources beat Wikipedians' translations, which beat
  machine translation. When machine-translating a quote, be confident it is accurate.

## Living people (วิกิพีเดีย:ชีวประวัติของบุคคลที่มีชีวิตอยู่)

- Applies to anyone born within the last 115 years with no confirmed death, and up to two years
  after a recent death. It covers every namespace, including talk pages, edit summaries,
  drafts, titles, categories and lists.
- Contentious material needs an inline citation to a reliable published source. Unsourced or
  poorly sourced contentious material is removed at once, not moved to the talk page.
- Conservative, restrained tone. No tabloid content, no trivia section. Criticism and praise
  only from reliable secondary sources.
- Unproven allegations are written as allegations, with the person's denial. People accused
  of crimes are presumed innocent until a court rules.
- No self-published sources about the person (unless the person published it, about
  themselves, with no third-party claims). No court records or public records for personal
  data. No contact details. Birth year only, when the person is marginally notable or objects.
- Categories, lists and infobox entries need sourced article text behind them.

## Titles (วิกิพีเดีย:ชื่อบทความ, rev 13323237)

- Thai script, the name reliable Thai sources use, the official Thai spelling over the
  transliteration rules. A noun or noun phrase (add การ or ความ to a verb or adjective).
- No official Thai name: transliterate by the existing rules, or keep the original name and
  tag the article `{{ชื่อภาษาอื่น}}`.
- English-style personal names: transliterate with a space between given and family name, put
  the original in parentheses in the lead. Chinese, Korean and Vietnamese names keep the
  surname first. Modern Japanese people: given name first.
- No civil titles (นาย, นาง, นางสาว, คุณหญิง), ranks or offices in a title. Royal and noble
  ranks stay. Roman numerals in names become "ที่ x".
- Countries take ประเทศ, capitals drop กรุง, lists start รายชื่อ or รายการ.
- Parentheses only for disambiguation, with a space before them. No `# < > [ ] | { } _`.
- Organisations not registered in Thailand: transliterate or translate the name, one or the other.

## Speedy deletion risks (วิกิพีเดีย:เงื่อนไขสำหรับการลบทันที, rev 12819232)

- **บ2**: Thai that is badly composed or unreadable, possibly machine translated. The main risk
  for this repo. A human makes every sentence readable before publishing.
- **บ1** too little context to identify the subject, **บ3** no real content (only links,
  categories, see also, templates), **บ5** no claim of significance for a person,
  organisation, web content or music: matter for new articles.
- **บ6**: a new article duplicating an existing Thai topic. Check Wikidata and Thai search
  before creating.
- **ท7**: an attack page or an entirely negative unsourced biography of a living person.
  **ท8**: unambiguous promotion.
- A creator or main author never removes a speedy-deletion tag from their own page. Contest it
  with the button and explain on the talk page.
